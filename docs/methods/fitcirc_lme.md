# `fitcirc_lme` — von Mises GLMM, exact EM

> **Cross-references.** Plain-language overview in the
> [README method section](../../README.md#fitcirc_lme--native-von-mises-mixed-effects-model)
> and the at-a-glance comparison in
> [`docs/backends.md`](../backends.md#fitcirc_lme--native-matlab).
> Per-function reference for the MATLAB callable: [`docs/functions.md`](../functions.md#fitcirc_lme).

This page is the full technical reference for the default backend. It
specifies the model, the EM algorithm, the convergence and monotonicity
guarantees, the cluster-robust standard-error construction, the joint Wald
machinery, and the goodness-of-fit metrics — at the level of detail a
statistician needs to know exactly what is being computed and what the
guarantees are.

---

## 1. Model

Let $y_{ij} \in (-\pi, \pi]$ denote the angular response of the $j$-th
observation on subject $i$, with $j = 1,\ldots,n_i$ and $i = 1,\ldots,m$.
Let $X_{ij} \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times p}$ be the fixed-effect design row,
$\beta \in \mathbb{R}^p$ the fixed-effect coefficient vector, and
$\phi_i \in (-\pi, \pi]$ a subject-level random intercept on the circle.

**Likelihood.** Conditional on $\beta, \phi_i$ and the response
concentration $\kappa > 0$,

$$
y_{ij} \mid \beta, \phi_i, \kappa  \sim  \text{vonMises}(\eta_{ij},\ \kappa),
\qquad \eta_{ij} = X_{ij}\beta + \phi_i.
$$

**Random-effect distribution.** Conditional on the subject-spread
concentration $\kappa_\phi > 0$,

$$
\phi_i \mid \kappa_\phi  \sim  \text{vonMises}(0,\ \kappa_\phi),
\quad i = 1,\ldots,m, \text{ independent.}
$$

The von Mises density is

$$
p_{\text{vM}}(y \mid \mu, \kappa)  =  \frac{1}{2\pi I_0(\kappa)}  \exp\bigl(\kappa \cos(y - \mu)\bigr),
$$

with $I_\nu(\cdot)$ the modified Bessel function of the first kind, order $\nu$.
The choice of a von Mises prior on $\phi_i$ (rather than the more common
wrapped Normal) is what makes the E-step closed-form: the von Mises is its
own conjugate under angular addition, so the posterior of $\phi_i$ given the
data and $(\beta, \kappa, \kappa_\phi)$ is again von Mises (Section 2).

**Identification.** Three sources of non-identification are removed by
construction:

- The $\pm\pi$ wrap is absorbed by centering the response by its empirical
  circular mean $\theta_\text{shift} = \arg(\overline{e^{i y}})$ before
  fitting; predictions are unshifted on output (see `circ_center.m`).
  Coefficients (other than the intercept) are invariant under this shift.
- The polynomial in $x = $ Age is reparameterized by an orthonormal-
  polynomial basis $P$ generated by `ortho_poly_basis`, so the
  design has $X'X = I$ on the polynomial block. The fitted curve and the
  joint Wald test are invariant under this nonsingular linear
  reparameterization; per-coefficient quantities live in the rotated basis.
- The intercept is absorbed into $\phi_i + \beta_0$. The reported intercept
  estimate $\hat\beta_0$ is identified by the constraint
  $\sum_i \hat\phi_i = 0$ on the posterior modes, which the EM produces
  automatically.

## 2. Closed-form E-step

Define, for each subject $i$, the de-meaned angles
$\alpha_{ij} = y_{ij} - X_{ij}\beta$ and the per-subject sufficient
statistics

$$
C_i  =  \sum_{j=1}^{n_i} \cos \alpha_{ij},
\qquad
S_i  =  \sum_{j=1}^{n_i} \sin \alpha_{ij}.
$$

Combining the likelihood factor $\exp\bigl(\kappa\sum_j \cos(\alpha_{ij} - \phi_i)\bigr)
= \exp\bigl(\kappa (C_i \cos\phi_i + S_i \sin\phi_i)\bigr)$ with the
prior factor $\exp(\kappa_\phi \cos\phi_i)$ gives a von Mises posterior

$$
\phi_i \mid y, \beta, \kappa, \kappa_\phi  \sim  \text{vonMises}(\mu_{i,\text{post}},\ K_{i,\text{post}}),
$$

with

$$
K_{i,\text{post}} = \sqrt{(\kappa C_i + \kappa_\phi)^2 + (\kappa S_i)^2}, \qquad \mu_{i,\text{post}} = \text{atan2}(\kappa S_i,\ \kappa C_i + \kappa_\phi).
$$

**Why this matters.** The M-step does not need the full posterior over
$\phi_i$; it needs only the *mean direction* $\mu_{i,\text{post}}$ and the
*mean resultant length*

$$
\rho_i  =  A(K_{i,\text{post}}), \qquad A(k)  =  \frac{I_1(k)}{I_0(k)} \in [0, 1).
$$

$\rho_i$ is the per-subject confidence weight: $\rho_i \to 1$ when subject
$i$'s posterior offset is sharply peaked (data is informative); $\rho_i \to 0$
when the posterior is nearly uniform.

---

## 3. M-step

### 3.1 Update of $\beta$

The complete-data score for $\beta$ at the current $(\mu_{i,\text{post}}, \rho_i)$ is

$$
\nabla_\beta \mathcal{Q}  =  \kappa \sum_{i, j} \rho_i  X_{ij}^\top \sin\bigl(y_{ij} - X_{ij}\beta - \mu_{i,\text{post}}\bigr).
$$

Setting this to zero yields a circular regression of the angles
$y_{ij} - \mu_{i,\text{post}}$ on $X_{ij}$ with observation weights $\rho_i$.
The implementation solves it by **iteratively reweighted least squares**
(IRLS): linearizing $\sin(r) \approx r$ at the current $\beta$, each
Newton step solves the weighted normal equations

$$
(X^\top W X)  \Delta\beta  =  X^\top W  r,
\qquad
W = \text{diag}\bigl(\rho_i\bigr)_{ij},
\quad
r = \bigl(y - X\beta - \mu_{\text{post}}\bigr)\ \text{wrapped to}\ (-\pi, \pi].
$$

Wrap-around inside $r$ is handled by mapping each residual through
$\text{atan2}(\sin\cdot, \cos\cdot)$.

### 3.2 Update of $\kappa$

The weighted mean resultant length of the residual angles is

$$
R  =  \frac{\bigl| \sum_{i,j} \rho_i  e^{i (y_{ij} - X_{ij}\beta - \mu_{i,\text{post}})} \bigr|}{\sum_{i,j} \rho_i}.
$$

Setting $A(\kappa) = R$ and inverting via the standard approximation of
Mardia & Jupp (2000) yields the M-step estimate of $\kappa$. The
implementation uses the rational approximation in
`local_kappa_from_R` (`fitcirc_lme.m`):

$$
\hat\kappa  \approx 
\begin{cases}
2R + R^3 + \tfrac{5R^5}{6} & R < 0.53, \\
-0.4 + 1.39R + 0.43/(1-R) & 0.53 \le R < 0.85, \\
1 / \bigl(R^3 - 4R^2 + 3R\bigr) & R \ge 0.85.
\end{cases}
$$

### 3.3 Update of $\kappa_\phi$

Let

$$
R_\phi  =  \frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m} \rho_i \cos \mu_{i,\text{post}}
$$

be the weighted mean resultant length of the estimated subject offsets
toward zero. The MAP update for $\kappa_\phi$ maximizes the exact
one-dimensional objective

$$
\ell(k)  =  m\bigl(R_\phi  k  -  \log I_0(k)\bigr)  +  \log \pi(k),
$$

where $\pi(k)$ is the prior on $\kappa_\phi$. The default prior is a
half-Cauchy on $k$ with scale $s = 8$:

$$
\pi(k)  \propto  \frac{1}{1 + (k/s)^2}, \qquad k \ge 0.
$$

Set through `KappaPhiPrior` (`'halfcauchy'` | `'halfnormal'` | `'none'`) and
`KappaPhiPriorScale`. An optional hard ceiling `KappaPhiMax` clips the
estimate at a finite value (default $\infty$).

**Why the prior.** When the fixed effects already absorb the population
trend, every $\mu_{i,\text{post}} \to 0$, so $R_\phi \to 1$ and the
maximum-likelihood estimate of $\kappa_\phi$ runs off to $+\infty$. That
sends $\log I_0(\kappa_\phi) \to +\infty$ in the marginal log-likelihood
and the EM stalls or returns `NaN`. The weakly-informative prior keeps the
estimate finite at the expense of a negligible shift in the typical
regime; see Gelman (2006) for the analogous half-Cauchy argument on
group-level standard deviations (the present case is the opposite
boundary, but the argument transfers).

If $R_\phi \le 0$, the optimum is at $k = 0$ (the prior is effectively
uniform on the circle and the offsets do not bunch around zero) and
$\kappa_\phi = 0$ is returned directly.

### 3.4 Monotonicity and backtracking

The three updates above are only approximate maximizers of the marginal
log-likelihood, so a full step can occasionally decrease it. The
implementation applies a **backtracking line search** along the proposed
parameter delta $(\Delta\beta, \Delta\kappa, \Delta\kappa_\phi)$: starting
from step size $s = 1$, $s \leftarrow s/2$ until the marginal log-
likelihood does not decrease. With backtracking the EM is **monotone**:

$$
\ell(\theta^{(t+1)})  \ge  \ell(\theta^{(t)}), \qquad t = 0, 1, 2, \ldots
$$

This is the property that lets the order-selection LRT be well posed:
warm-starting order $k$ from order $k{-}1$'s converged solution plus the
monotone update guarantees $\ell_k^\star \ge \ell_{k-1}^\star$, so the
nested $\chi^2$ statistic $-2(\ell_{k-1}^\star - \ell_k^\star)$ is
non-negative and the LRT cascade does not spuriously terminate.

Convergence is declared when the relative change in $\ell$ between EM
iterations drops below `Tol` (default $10^{-6}$), or after `MaxIter`
iterations (default $500$).

## 4. Marginal log-likelihood

After integrating out $\phi_i$ exactly,

$$
\log p(y_i \mid \beta, \kappa, \kappa_\phi) = \log I_0(K_{i,\text{post}}) - n_i \log(2\pi\, I_0(\kappa)) - \log I_0(\kappa_\phi).
$$

Sum over $i$ for the cohort-level marginal log-likelihood. The reported
`LogLikelihood` field is **unpenalized** (the prior on $\kappa_\phi$ does
not enter), so nested LRTs and AIC/BIC across orders are computed in the
same likelihood family.

## 5. Standard errors: cluster-robust sandwich

### 5.1 Construction

For inference on $\beta$, treat each subject as a cluster and form the
sandwich estimator of $\text{Var}(\hat\beta)$.

**Bread** (Fisher / expected information): from the von Mises score
identity $\text{Var}(\sin\epsilon) = 1 - A(\kappa)^2$ at the mode and
$\mathbb{E}\bigl[\partial_\beta (\partial_\beta \mathcal{Q})^\top\bigr]
= \kappa A(\kappa) \sum_{ij} \rho_i X_{ij}^\top X_{ij}$, define

$$
H  =  \hat\kappa  A(\hat\kappa)  \sum_{i,j} \hat\rho_i  X_{ij}^\top X_{ij}.
$$

$H$ is positive-definite as long as the design has full rank on the
$\rho_i$-weighted observations.

**Meat** (empirical covariance of cluster scores): the score contribution
from cluster $i$ at the converged $(\hat\beta, \hat\kappa, \hat\rho_i,
\hat\mu_{i,\text{post}})$ is

$$
U_i  =  \hat\kappa  \hat\rho_i \sum_{j=1}^{n_i} X_{ij}^\top \sin\bigl(y_{ij} - X_{ij}\hat\beta - \hat\mu_{i,\text{post}}\bigr).
$$

The meat is

$$
B  =  \sum_{i=1}^{m} U_i U_i^\top.
$$

**Sandwich.** The cluster-robust covariance is

$$
\widehat{\text{Var}}(\hat\beta)  =  c_{m,n}  H^{-1}  B  H^{-1},
$$

with the small-sample correction

$$
c_{m,n}  =  \frac{m}{m-1}\cdot\frac{n - 1}{n - p}, \qquad n = \sum_i n_i.
$$

### 5.2 Inference

Per-coefficient tests use Student's $t$ on $\nu = m - 1$ degrees of
freedom:

$$
T_k  =  \frac{\hat\beta_k}{\sqrt{\widehat{\text{Var}}(\hat\beta)_{kk}}}, \qquad T_k  \overset{H_0}{\sim}  t_{\nu}.
$$

Joint Wald tests on a block of $r$ coefficients use an $F$-statistic with
$(r, m - r)$ degrees of freedom (or equivalently $\chi^2_r$ in the
large-$m$ limit). For the omnibus age block, the contrast indices live in
`mdl.ContrastIndex.x_age` and are constructed automatically by
`circ_fit_fitcirc` from the design column names; see `joint_test.m` for
the dispatch.

### 5.3 Caveats

- Sandwich SE under-coverage is documented for **very small clusters**
  ($n_i \in \{1, 2\}$); the asymptotic argument needs the cluster-level
  score covariance to converge, which is rough with two observations per
  cluster. Coverage at $n_i \ge 4$ is essentially nominal.
- The bread uses the *expected* (Fisher) information rather than the
  observed Hessian, because the latter can be indefinite near the
  $\kappa_\phi$ boundary; the expected form is always positive-definite.

## 6. Polynomial order selection

With `Select = true`, the toolbox sweeps $k = 0, 1, \ldots, \text{MaxOrder}$
and applies a **step-up likelihood-ratio test** at $\alpha = 0.05$:

- Fit order $0$; record $\ell_0^\star$.
- For $k = 1, 2, \ldots$: warm-start from $\hat\theta_{k-1}^\star$, fit
  order $k$; test $H_0$: order is $k-1$ via $-2(\ell_{k-1}^\star - \ell_k^\star) \sim \chi^2_{\Delta_k}$
  where $\Delta_k$ is the number of new fixed-effect coefficients
  introduced at order $k$. If $p < 0.05$, accept order $k$ and continue.
  Otherwise stop and report $k - 1$.

Warm-starting plus monotone EM guarantees $\ell_k^\star \ge \ell_{k-1}^\star$
by construction, so the test statistic is $\ge 0$ — the LRT cannot
spuriously fail because of a non-monotone likelihood.

The full per-order audit (LL, $R^2_\text{circ}$, the criterion value, and
the row that was selected) is in the returned `OrderTable`.

## 7. Goodness of fit

### 7.1 Marginal $R^2_\text{circ}$ (population-level)

Define the circular sum-of-squares decomposition on the population-mean
predictions $\hat y^\text{pop}_{ij} = X_{ij}\hat\beta$ (the fixed-effect
prediction; random effects integrated out):

$$
\text{SSE}_\text{circ}  =  \sum_{i, j} \bigl(1 - \cos(y_{ij} - \hat y^\text{pop}_{ij})\bigr),
$$

$$
\text{SST}_\text{circ}  =  \sum_{i, j} \bigl(1 - \cos(y_{ij} - \bar y^\text{circ})\bigr),
$$

with $\bar y^\text{circ} = \arg(\overline{e^{i y}})$ the circular mean.
Then

$$
R^2_\text{circ,m}  =  1 - \frac{\text{SSE}_\text{circ}}{\text{SST}_\text{circ}}.
$$

This quantity answers "how much of the angular variation does the
population trend (fixed effects) alone explain?" It is comparable across
all four backends because the formula uses only the angle scale and
fixed-effect predictions.

### 7.2 Conditional $R^2_\text{circ}$

The conditional $R^2_\text{circ}$ lifts the marginal value by the
contribution of the subject random intercept, via a Nakagawa–Schielzeth
(2013) ICC adjustment adapted to the von Mises GLMM. Define the per-
component circular variances

$$
V_\alpha  =  1 - A(\hat\kappa_\phi), \qquad V_\varepsilon  =  1 - A(\hat\kappa).
$$

(They are circular variances by definition: $V \in [0, 1]$, with $V = 0$
when the corresponding concentration is infinite — no variation — and
$V = 1$ when it is zero — uniform on the circle.) The intra-class
correlation among the two is

$$
\text{ICC}  =  \frac{V_\alpha}{V_\alpha + V_\varepsilon},
$$

and the conditional $R^2_\text{circ}$ is

$$
R^2_\text{circ,c} = R^2_\text{circ,m} + \text{ICC} \cdot (1 - R^2_\text{circ,m}).
$$

**Limit case.** When $\hat\kappa_\phi \to \infty$, the EM is signaling
"no subject random-intercept variance" (the response carries no
persistent subject baseline). In that limit $V_\alpha \to 0$,
$\text{ICC} \to 0$, and $R^2_\text{circ,c} = R^2_\text{circ,m}$, which is
the correct conservative reporting.

### 7.3 MAE

The mean absolute angular error is

$$
\text{MAE}_\text{angular}  =  \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i, j} \bigl|y_{ij} - \hat y^\text{pop}_{ij}\bigr|_\text{wrap},
$$

with $|r|_\text{wrap} = |\text{atan2}(\sin r, \cos r)|$ ensuring residuals
are computed modulo $2\pi$.

### 7.4 AIC / BIC

Reported as $-2 \ell + 2p$ and $-2 \ell + p \log n$ respectively, with
$p$ the number of fixed-effect coefficients and $n = \sum_i n_i$. The
prior on $\kappa_\phi$ does not enter either expression.

## 8. Numerical notes

- All Bessel function evaluations use `besseli`. For $\kappa \gtrsim 100$,
  $\log I_0(\kappa) \approx \kappa - \tfrac{1}{2}\log(2\pi\kappa)$ to
  machine precision; the implementation uses this asymptotic form to
  avoid `Inf`.
- The IRLS step matrix $X^\top W X$ has $X'X = I$ on the polynomial block
  by construction (orthonormal basis), so the inversion is well-conditioned
  even at high polynomial orders.
- The atan2 angular-residual mapping is implemented as
  `wrap = @(x) ((x + pi) - 2*pi*floor((x + pi)/(2*pi))) - pi` rather than
  through atan2 directly to avoid the cost of repeated trig calls.
- The marginal log-likelihood is computed once per EM iteration (for the
  backtracking decision and for convergence). No closed-form gradient is
  used; the EM updates themselves are exact maximizers of the complete-
  data log-likelihood, so gradient-based outer optimization is not needed.

## 9. Assumptions and when they bite

| Assumption | When it matters | How the toolbox handles it |
|---|---|---|
| Subject offsets are von Mises (not wrapped Normal) | Mild; the two families are close when $\kappa_\phi$ is moderate. Matters when $\kappa_\phi \to 0$ (offsets nearly uniform), in which case the EM degenerates to a fixed-effects-only fit and the conditional $R^2$ collapses to the marginal. | Reported automatically: $V_\alpha = 1$ when $\hat\kappa_\phi = 0$, $\text{ICC} = 1/(1 + V_\varepsilon)$. |
| Single $(1\|group)$ random intercept; no random slopes | When the subject random structure is more complex (e.g. random electrode-by-Age slopes), the residual concentration $\hat\kappa$ absorbs the unmodelled variance and the sandwich SE may underestimate. | Documented limitation. Switch to a Bayesian backend (`brms`) for richer random-effects structures. |
| Trajectory fits within one revolution of the response | The von Mises mean is a single circular point. A trajectory that genuinely sweeps more than $2\pi$ across the predictor range cannot be represented. | Documented limitation. Switch to `bpnreg`, which represents the mean via a projection of a 2-D latent Gaussian and so can sweep all of $2\pi$. |
| Clusters are large enough that the empirical cluster-score covariance converges | Sandwich SEs under-cover at $n_i \in \{1, 2\}$. | Documented; small-cluster regimes should use the resample-based options (`Resample = 'cboot'` or `'sub80'`). |
| The polynomial order is reasonably bounded | Higher orders extrapolate wildly at the edges of the predictor range. | Default `MaxOrder = 2`; raise consciously. |
